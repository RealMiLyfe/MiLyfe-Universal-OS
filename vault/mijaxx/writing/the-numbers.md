# The Numbers — Jacksonville Research Data

All data from the Growth Paradox research series (11 chapters). These are the facts.

## Budget (Chapter 2)

- Jacksonville General Fund (FY2026-27): ~$2.03 Billion
- Total city budget: ~$5.3 Billion/year
- Capital Improvement Program: $586 Million
- Pension unfunded liability: $2.9-3.2 Billion
- Annual pension cost (ADC): $280-320 Million
- JEA transfer to general fund: ~$114-120 Million/year
- Pre-committed spending (pension + JEA + legacy): ~70 cents of every dollar
- Per capita spending: ~$1,700 (below peer cities)
- Nashville per capita: ~$2,100
- Charlotte per capita: ~$1,950
- Austin per capita: ~$2,400
- Public Safety share of general fund: 52-55%
- Public Health share: 3-4%
- Zero drawn from reserves (second consecutive year — Deegan claims credit)

## Affordability (Chapter 3)

- Median home price increase (2015-2024): +80%
- Wage increase same period: +32%
- Gap: housing growing 2.5x faster than wages
- Cost-burdened renters (spending 30%+ on housing): 47-52%
- Insurance premium increases: 75-130%
- Jacksonville's historic affordability advantage: evaporating

## Public Safety (Chapter 4)

- JSO officers per 1,000 residents: 1.7-1.8 (national target: 2.2+)
- Coverage area: 874 square miles (largest in contiguous US)
- Response times in growth areas: 12-18 minutes
- Homicide clearance rate: 50-60% (below national average)
- Pedestrian danger: Top 5 most dangerous cities nationally

## Infrastructure (Chapter 5)

- Active septic tanks: 55,000-65,000
- Septic-to-sewer pace at current rate: 50+ years to complete
- Stormwater infrastructure need: $1 Billion+
- Road network: deteriorating faster than repaired
- Sea level rise projection: +0.5-1.5 feet by 2050

## Development (Chapter 6)

- REV grants to developers: $15-40 Million/year
- CRA tax diversion: $30-50 Million/year
- Impact fees: below true infrastructure cost
- Donor-incentive correlation: documented in public records

## Transportation (Chapter 7)

- JTA transit ridership: 11-13 trips per capita per year
- Car-dependent commuters: 85%+
- No viable transit alternative for most residents
- Pedestrian fatality ranking: Top 5 nationally

## Education (Chapter 8)

- Teacher vacancy crisis: ongoing
- Childcare cost: $12-15K/year per infant
- Graduate retention vs peer cities: poor
- ACE scores (Adverse Childhood Experiences): primary predictor of outcomes

## Environment (Chapter 9)

- 55K septic tanks → nitrogen → algae blooms in the St. Johns River
- Urban heat island: growing
- No formal resilience plan published
- Sea level data shows accelerating risk

## Governance (Chapter 10)

- Consolidated government structure: built for 1968, serving 2027
- OIG (Office of Inspector General): underfunded relative to $5.3B operation
- Independent authorities: limited public accountability
- Transparency: technically available, functionally opaque
