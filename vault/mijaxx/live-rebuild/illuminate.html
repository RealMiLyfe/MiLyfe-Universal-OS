The Growth Paradox — 11 Chapters

Eleven years of independent research into how Jacksonville actually works — budget, housing, policing, infrastructure, development. Don't take a speech. Take the numbers.

01The Growth Paradox

The city grows — and life gets harder. Why prosperity on paper doesn't reach the street.

02Where the Money Goes

~$5.3B a year. 52–55% to reaction, 3–4% to prevention. ~70 cents of every dollar pre-committed before a single new decision.

03The Affordability Lie

Homes up 80% while wages rose 32%. Half of renters cost-burdened. The historic affordability advantage: evaporating.

04Thin Blue Math

1.7–1.8 officers per 1,000 residents across 874 square miles. 12–18 minute responses in growth areas. Math, not blame.

05The Infrastructure Debt Bomb

55,000–65,000 septic tanks. $1B+ in stormwater need. Roads decaying faster than repair. Sea rising 0.5–1.5 ft by 2050.

06The Development Machine

$15–40M/year in REV grants, $30–50M in CRA diversion, impact fees below true cost. Follow the public records.

07Stuck

85%+ car-dependent. 11–13 transit trips per capita per year. Top 5 nationally for pedestrian deaths.

08The Pipeline Problem

How the systems that should lift kids up keep feeding the same outcomes — and what a contribution pipeline looks like instead.

09The River Doesn't Lie

What the St. Johns tells us about stewardship, neglect, and who the city is really managed for.

10The Machine

How the pieces connect into one self-protecting system — and the single leverage point that changes it.

11Jacksonville Deserves Better

The closing argument: prevention pays more than crime, and a good neighbor must be worth more than a bad one.
