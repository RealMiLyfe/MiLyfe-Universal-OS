From the Bottom to the Ballot

"The city arrested me 11 times, harassed me hundreds of times. I'm running to lead it. Not for revenge. For the lost boys and girls who are still out there."

1986 — Born

Born January 11 in Miami, Florida. Mother passes in 1991. Father passes June 5, 1994. Raised by family in Jacksonville from age 8.

2003 — The Fall

First arrest at 17 in Jacksonville. What follows: 11 Jacksonville arrests as an adult, 30+ total, homelessness from 2006–2008 — breaking into apartments to eat and shower. Surviving.

2015 — The Work Begins

Age 29 — the age his mother died. He has a daughter. On June 5, exactly 21 years to the day after his father passed, he writes everything wrong about himself in a notebook, defines "UNDERSTAND," and asks one question: "Why do people fight?"

2015–2026 — 11 Years, $0

Eleven years of independent research. Not one dollar earned from it. Sold cars, sold computers, ate from trash cans, bathed with a water hose at a storage unit. Lost relatives who counted him out. Nearly ended it twice. Survived both times.

Aug 2025 — Brutalized in a Crosswalk

Brutalized by police in a crosswalk. Emergency room visit. A $2.5M civil rights demand letter is prepared and ready to file.

Jul 2026 — MiLyfe Founded

The MiLyfe platform is founded. Then on July 29, at 40 years old, he registers to vote for the first time in his life — to put his OWN name on the ballot.

2027 — The Ballot

Petition. Qualifying. Primary March 9. General May 18. One city saved — then the country.

The Turning Point, In His Words

"I was done. Either this is revealed to me — what this project is for, why I built it, who it serves — or I quit. I give up. I felt betrayed by the universe. I cursed the creator. Then it was revealed. And I cried. Because for the first time in 20 years as an entrepreneur, I had something with no bullshit on it. Something I could be 100% proud of. Something honest. I can just be honest through my brokenness."

For His Daughter

For years she asked: "Is Frankenstein done yet?" Then she stopped asking. The silence was the weight.

"It's done. I built it for you."
