The MiLyfe Story

From Trash Cans to City Hall

The proof is public. The work was built in the open and timestamped on a blockchain in 2017. See The Receipts — verify it yourself.

The timeline

  • 1986 (Jan 11): Born in Miami
  • 1991 (Mar 28): Mother passes (age 5)
  • 1994 (Jun 5): Father passes (age 8)
  • ~2003+: Arrests begin — first arrest at age 17 in Jacksonville. 11 Jacksonville arrests as an adult, plus multiple in Miami.
  • 2006–2008: Homeless in Jacksonville. Breaking into apartments to eat/shower.
  • 2008+: Criminal record accumulates (30+ arrests total)
  • ~2015: Age 29, nervous about mortality (mother died at 29). Has a daughter. Wants better.
  • 2015 (Jun 5): STARTS THE WORK — 21 years to the day after father died. Writes everything wrong about himself in a notebook. Defines “UNDERSTAND.” Asks: “WHY DO PEOPLE FIGHT?”
  • 2015–2026: 11 years of independent research. No income from this work. Sold cars, computers to survive. Ate from trash cans. Bathed with water hose. Lost relatives and friends who counted him out. Lost communication with daughter’s mom. Daughter stopped asking about Frankenstein and only texts every blue moon. Unable to pay child support. Nearly ended it all twice.
  • 2025 (Aug 25): Brutalized by police in crosswalk. Emergency room visit. $2.5M demand letter ready to be filed.
  • 2026: Cuts off relatives who doubted him. “INDEFINITELY. NEVER COMING BACK.”
  • 2026 (Jul 24): MiLyfe founded
  • 2026 (Jul 29): REGISTERS TO VOTE (first time, age 40) TO PUT OWN NAME ON BALLOT. 165 days to qualifying.

The turning point

“I was done. Either this is revealed to me — what this project is for, why I built it, who it serves — or I quit. I give up. I was past feeling stupid, dumb, devalued. I felt betrayed by the universe. I cursed the creator.

Then it was revealed.

And I cried. Because for the first time in 20 years as an entrepreneur, I had something with no bullshit on it. Something I could be 100% proud of. Something honest.

I can just be honest through my brokenness.”

The daughter moment

For years she asked: “Is Frankenstein done yet?”
Then she stopped asking.
The silence was the weight.
